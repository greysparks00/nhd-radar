Before Radar

Radar stands for radio detection and ranging. A radar system sends radio waves toward an object and receives the waves that bounce back. The time taken for the signal to return helps show how far away the object is. This lets people find objects without seeing them directly. It uses radio waves rather than sound waves. A receiver collects the returning signal so the equipment can display information.

In the late 1800s, Heinrich Hertz showed that radio waves could reflect off metal. In 1904, Christian Hulsmeyer used this idea to make a device that detected ships. These early experiments helped build the science behind later radar systems.

Before radar, Britain also used large sound mirrors to listen for aircraft engines. These mirrors show that early warning was already a problem before World War II. As aircraft became a greater threat, finding approaching planes became more important to national defense.

Radar did not come from one person working alone. Scientists in different countries added to its development. The important change came when governments supported the research and connected detection equipment to a larger warning system.
